01 / My take

Six ideas I started from.

Radio was good at one thing: it was there the moment you reached for it. Everything below is me trying to keep that.

01

Radio should just start

No sign-up, and no home feed to scroll first.

A radio has no loading screen. You turn the knob and there is sound. So the app opens on your stations, one tap starts one, and the audio fades in softly instead of arriving with a pop.

  • Resume on launch A three-second countdown starts your last station, and you can cancel it.
  • It keeps going If a stream drops, the app keeps trying to reconnect on its own until you press pause.
  • No gap between stations On iPhone the next station loads quietly while the old one plays, then they cross-fade.
02

Controls should feel like a radio

Keys you press, not icons you hope you hit.

I wanted the player to feel physical. The transport keys press in and give a small vibration. Volume is a strip of ten rising bars you can tap, drag or hold, like the meter on a stereo.

  • Previous and next say where they go Each key shows the name of the station it will switch to.
  • Haptics throughout Taps, volume steps, errors and confirmations, all with a quiet buzz you can switch off.
03

The station is the star

The app should step aside for the thing you came to hear.

Every station has its own logo, frequency and city, and that is what the player is made of. The animated backdrop takes its colours from the logo, and the text flips between light and dark so it is always readable.

  • Continue listening A card for your last station with one-tap resume, over a slow blur in the logo’s colours.
  • Station details Genres, language, stream format, and a link to the station’s own website.
04

Nobody should have to touch the volume

Switching stations shouldn’t mean grabbing the slider.

Stations broadcast at wildly different levels. Go from one mastered loud to a quiet talk station and your ears pay for it. So a small server measures how loud each station is, and the app turns the loud ones down to match.

The server only reports a number. Your audio comes straight from the station, untouched.

05

Quiet, and minding its own business

A radio app has no reason to know who you are.

There are no accounts, no ads and no tracking. Your history and settings live on your phone and nowhere else. And there is a sleep timer that fades out gently instead of cutting you off.

  • Private by design What the app stores, and how to delete it, is on the privacy policy page.
06

Calm, minimal, a little Japanese

Flat, thin lines, and room to breathe.

The look is flat and clean: thin borders, no heavy shadows, rounded type. I borrowed from Japanese and anime design, which is why the greeting changes with the time of day (おはよう, こんにちは, こんばんは) and the Continue listening card has a fine film grain.

  • Light and dark Follow the system, or pick one, with a lime accent.
  • Native where it counts The real iOS tab bar, native menus and large titles.
02 / Details

Small things I cared about.

The kind of thing you only notice when it is missing.

  • Soft starts Every start fades in. No pop, no sudden blast.
  • It reconnects itself A stalled or dropped stream is retried until you press pause. Pauses from you, the lock screen or a phone call are respected.
  • Lock screen controls Station name, details and logo on the lock screen, Control Center and the Android notification.
  • Background playback Audio keeps going when you leave the app or lock your phone.
  • Silent mode On iPhone it keeps playing with the ringer switch muted, unless you turn that off.
  • Asking first If something is playing, pressing Play on another station asks before it switches.
  • Saved logos Logos are kept on the phone after the first time, so they don’t download again. Settings shows the space they use and clears it in one tap.
  • Morning radio On iPhone, a Shortcuts automation can open the app and play your last station at a time you choose.
03 / Left out

What I left out.

Restraint is a design decision too.

  • No accounts There is nothing to sign into and no cloud sync. History and settings stay on your phone.
  • No ads, no tracking Nothing to sell, and nothing in the app that watches what you listen to.
  • No downloads It is live radio, and only live radio.
  • Not the current song The app shows the station you are listening to, not the track playing. That is a gap in this version.
  • Mostly Uganda The catalogue is built around Ugandan stations, with some favourites from elsewhere: Heart UK, K-LOVE, US hip-hop and country from Tennessee.
04 / Under the hood

Small on purpose.

A phone app, a small API, and two quiet workers.

The app is built with Expo and React Native for iPhone and Android. It gets its stations from a small Flask API, so new stations appear without an app update. Two background workers keep that list honest: one checks that every stream really plays, and the other measures how loud each one is.
